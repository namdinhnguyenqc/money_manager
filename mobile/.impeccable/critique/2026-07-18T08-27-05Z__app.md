## Anti-patterns verdict

The app has a credible task-first Home and solid money-flow foundations, but Reports reads like a generated premium analytics template: repeated bento cards, colored icon badges, redundant summaries, and too many sections with equal authority. The deterministic detector returned zero findings across 50 TS/TSX files, but its CSS-oriented rules do not fully understand React Native StyleSheet objects. Manual evidence found thin-border plus wide-shadow combinations in `components/ui/KPICard.tsx` and `app/invoice/bulk.tsx`.

## What works

- Home prioritizes one financial summary, urgent exceptions and direct actions.
- Payment prevents overpayment and retains invoice context.
- Deposit keeps room, amount and destination wallet visible near confirmation.

## Priority issues

### P1 — Reports can imply unsupported financial certainty

Rolling periods include future dates via `Math.abs`; paid rooms can include rooms without invoices; paid amount can fall back to rent; year options are hard-coded. Derive payment state only from positive invoice evidence, bound dates through now, generate years dynamically and disclose coverage.

### P1 — Invoice creation collects values it does not persist

Due date and note appear in the UI but are missing from submission. Persist them end-to-end or remove the controls.

### P1 — Errors masquerade as valid empty or zero states

Invoices, Reports, Facilities and Deposit swallow failures or continue with empty data. Add persistent recovery with Retry, cached-data disclosure and safe navigation.

### P2 — Reports overloads instead of prioritizing decisions

Lead with a small set of decision metrics and exceptions. Move utility and multi-year detail into progressive disclosure.

### P2 — Accessibility and system drift

Muted contrast, undersized touch targets, inconsistent primary color, arbitrary toast motion/z-index and broad shadows violate the documented system. Consolidate tokens, enforce 44px targets, add accessible labels and reduced motion.

## Persona red flags

- Owner on the go: long forms, raw dates, no draft persistence and fleeting success feedback.
- First-time landlord: silent first-item defaults, jargon and modal-only validation.
- High-volume operator: unbounded horizontal selectors, undisclosed 1000-row report limit and non-virtualized nested lists.

## Minor observations

Invoice export replaces the whole list with skeletons; transaction filters mutate behind an Apply modal; a wallet row has a false chevron affordance; StatusBadge key naming drifts; Reports labels a rolling 365-day period as “Năm nay.”

## Questions

- Should paid status ever be inferred without a positive payment record?
- Which three exceptions matter most to an owner with 100 rooms and ten seconds?
- Which money-moving defaults require explicit confirmation?
